# C9 — Macro Vintages and Regimes

Macro observations preserve release identity, scheduled and actual publication time, first byte, durable persistence, strategy availability, vintage, revision chain, unit, seasonal treatment and source hash.

A historical context sees only values available by its knowledge cutoff. Current revised history cannot replace the originally published vintage. Release dates and website availability are distinct fields.

Surprise measures record the expectation source, timestamp, distribution and revision policy. Consensus estimates and nowcasts are derived inputs rather than official facts.

Regime outputs are probabilistic and versioned. Candidate dimensions include growth, inflation, rates, liquidity, credit, volatility and cross-asset stress. Regime methods are compared against simple transparent baselines and may abstain when probabilities are diffuse or outside support.

Every macro event links to an exposure graph and competing transmission hypotheses. Correlation is not labeled causal without the C6 identification controls.
